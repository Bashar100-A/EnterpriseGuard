# ADIE — Objection Handling

**Rule:** Don't argue. Acknowledge. Ask a question. Let them talk.

---

## Objection 1 — "We already have a GRC platform."

> "Understood. GRC platforms manage compliance workflows — they don't
> produce an independently verifiable artifact for a specific decision.
> The question is: can your regulator verify a decision without your
> cooperation? If yes, you don't need us."

**Then:** Wait. Let them answer.

---

## Objection 2 — "Our logs are already sufficient."

> "Maybe. When was the last time a regulator asked you to prove a
> log hasn't been altered? If they did, could you prove it? Without
> your IT team's help?"

**Then:** Ask how they handled it.

---

## Objection 3 — "This is too technical for us."

> "Agreed — that's the point. The verifier is 179 lines. Your
> auditor's IT can read it in 10 minutes. The math is standard:
> SHA-256, RSA-2048, RFC 6962. All published."

**Then:** Offer to show the one-pager.

---

## Objection 4 — "We don't use AI for high-risk decisions."

> "That's common. But regulators are asking about *any* AI-driven
> decision now, not just high-risk. And 'scheduled for 2027' means
> your 2026 audit will be the first one where this gets asked."

**Then:** Wait.

---

## Objection 5 — "Our regulator doesn't require this yet."

> "Right. They don't. But your next audit prep will take 3 weeks
> anyway. The question is: does this make that 3 weeks shorter?"

**Then:** Ask about their last audit prep.

---

## Objection 6 — "We can build this ourselves."

> "You could. It's ~200 lines of Python plus a schema. The question
> is whether that's a good use of your team's time — versus using
> something already tested and free."

**Then:** Wait.

---

## Objection 7 — "Startups like this always disappear."

> "Fair concern. That's why the verifier is standalone — it works
> even if we disappear. The specification is public. Your certificate
> stays verifiable forever."

**Then:** Show the offline test.

---

## Objection 8 — "Our legal team won't approve."

> "We don't ask for legal approval. We don't claim compliance. We
> provide a technical artifact. Your legal team decides how to use it."

**Then:** Offer to send a statement they can share with legal.

---

## Objection 9 — "The math seems complex."

> "It's not. Three steps: hash the content, sign the hash, verify
> the signature. RFC 6962 is just a specific way of combining hashes."

**Then:** Show the verifier source.

---

## Objection 10 — "Come back in 6 months."

> "Understood. Can I ask: what would change in 6 months that would
> make this more relevant? Or is it a timing issue?"

**Then:** Record the answer verbatim. It's data.

---

## Universal Rule

If they say "no" three times in different ways — **stop asking**.
Thank them. Ask if they know someone else who might be interested.

Never push.
