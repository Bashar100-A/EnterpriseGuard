# ADIE — Pricing Thinking Framework

**Status:** Internal thinking. NOT for external sharing.

**Rule:** No prices are set. Only frameworks for thinking.

---

## What Are We Selling?

Not "software". Not "compliance". Not "AI".

**We sell: reduced audit prep time for a specific decision type.**

The value unit is: **hours saved per audited decision**.

---

## Three Pricing Models to Consider

### Model 1 — Per-Decision Price

- Client pays X per certificate issued.
- Pros: aligns with usage.
- Cons: hard to estimate upfront; requires metering.

### Model 2 — Per-Pilot Price

- Fixed fee for the 14-day pilot.
- Pros: simple; easy to approve.
- Cons: no ongoing revenue.

### Model 3 — Per-Auditor-Seat

- Annual fee per auditor using the verifier.
- Pros: recurring; scalable.
- Cons: low initial revenue; needs many seats.

**For Gate 3 pilot:** Use Model 2 only. Defer Model 1 and 3.

---

## What Determines the Price?

Do NOT set price before knowing:

1. **Current cost:** how much does one audit prep currently cost (in hours × salary)?
2. **Value delivered:** how many hours does ADIE save?
3. **Alternative cost:** what does the client currently pay for GRC / logs / audit tooling?
4. **Budget authority:** can the Audit Lead approve this, or does it need CFO?
5. **Procurement friction:** is there a minimum threshold below which no procurement is needed?

**Until we know these five, any price is a guess.**

---

## Rough Value Calculation (Client Side)

Example (not a commitment):

- Audit prep: 3 days × 8 hours = 24 hours.
- Auditor cost: €80 / hour fully loaded.
- Current cost per decision: €1,920.
- If ADIE saves 40%: €768 saved per decision.
- If client audits 50 decisions/year: €38,400 saved / year.

**This is the CEILING.** Actual price should be well below.

---

## What We Should Ask in the Call

1. "How many AI decisions do you typically audit per year?"
2. "What's the rough preparation time per decision?"
3. "Do you have a budget for audit tooling? If yes, roughly how much?"
4. "What's your approval threshold — below what amount can you approve without CFO?"

**Do NOT ask price directly in the first call.**

---

## Anchor Values (for internal calibration only)

These are just thinking anchors. Do NOT quote externally.

- **Below €1,000 total:** probably no procurement friction, easy pilot.
- **€1,000 – €5,000:** minor friction, likely one approval level.
- **€5,000 – €20,000:** standard enterprise pilot, 2–3 approval levels.
- **€20,000 – €50,000:** requires CFO visibility.
- **€50,000+:** full procurement process, months.

**For the first pilot:** aim for the lowest reasonable value that is
non-zero. The goal is a signature, not revenue.

---

## What "Paid Pilot" Means in the Contract

- 14 days duration.
- Fixed fee.
- Deliverable: signed measurement note.
- No exclusivity.
- No IP transfer.
- Either party can terminate with notice.

**The point of the fee is NOT money.** It is commitment. A paid pilot
tests whether the client will invest resources, not just time.
