# ADIE — Gate 3 Playbook (Customer Truth)

**Status:** Pre-launch (internal)
**Purpose:** Prepare for Gate 3 — talking to real auditors about the
POC.
**Rule:** Gate 3 is qualitative. We do not invent numbers. We measure
after.

---

## §1 — What Gate 3 Tests

Gate 3 answers one question:

> **Does an actual Internal Audit Lead, at an actual EU bank, find
> the certificate materially useful in their work?**

Not: "is it interesting?" Not: "is it cool?" But: **"would you use
this in your next audit?"**

---

## §2 — Who We Target

**Primary:**
- Title: Internal Audit Lead / Audit Manager / Head of Internal Audit
- Institution: EU bank (5,000–50,000 employees)
- Domain: Technology risk, model risk, or AI audit
- Geography: EU (DORA applies)

**Secondary (if primary unavailable):**
- Model Risk Officer at same institution
- Compliance officer with audit responsibilities

**Reject for Gate 3:**
- CISO (different pain)
- CTO (different pain)
- AI Governance Officer (different role)
- External regulator (not a buyer)

---

## §3 — Where to Find Them

**LinkedIn (most effective):**
- Search: "Internal Audit" + "bank" + "EU city"
- Filter: 5,000+ employee companies
- Look at: their recent posts about DORA, AI Act, audit

**Industry events (if accessible):**
- IIA (Institute of Internal Auditors) chapters
- Local banking associations
- Webinars on DORA / AI Act

**Warm introductions (highest success):**
- Ask friends in banking
- Ask your developer friend (maybe they know someone)
- Ask LinkedIn connections for a "second-degree" intro

**Do NOT:**
- Cold-email 1000 auditors
- Use automated outreach tools
- Send generic messages

**Target: 10 warm leads. Expect 3-5 responses. Need 2-3 calls.**

---

## §4 — The Message (LinkedIn)

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm working on a small project that helps auditors verify AI
> decisions independently. DORA-relevant.
>
> Would you have 20 minutes to look at it and tell me whether it
> actually helps?
>
> No pitch. Just want a real auditor's opinion.
>
> Happy to share a one-page summary first.

**Rules:**
- Short.
- No jargon.
- No claims.
- Ask for opinion, not sale.
- Offer to send summary first (respects their time).

---

## §5 — The 30-Minute Call Structure

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Greeting + permission to record (for notes) |
| 3–8 min | Ask about their current audit prep process |
| 8–15 min | Show the one-pager + the certificate |
| 15–25 min | 8 questions (see interview-script.md) |
| 25–30 min | Ask: "Would you try a pilot?" / Close |

**Rules:**
- Do not pitch.
- Do not defend.
- Take notes verbatim (quotes, not summaries).
- If they say "no" — say thank you, ask why, hang up.

---

## §6 — What to Measure

After each call, record (before the next call):

| Metric | How |
|---|---|
| Time they spend per audit prep | Their number, not ours |
| Time they think we could save | Their estimate |
| "Would you use this?" | Yes / No / Maybe |
| "Would you pay?" | Yes / No / Unknown |
| Specific pain named | Quote |

---

## §7 — Success Criteria (Pre-Committed)

**After 3 calls:**

| Outcome | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **PASS** | ≥ 2 of 3 say "would use" AND ≥ 1 says "would pay" |
| **PARTIAL** | ≥ 1 of 3 says "would use" OR finds it interesting |
| **FAIL** | < 1 of 3 says "would use" |

**No interpretation after the fact.**

---

## §8 — What Gate 3 Does NOT Do

- Does not commit to a pilot.
- Does not set a price.
- Does not promise compliance.
- Does not change the POC.
- Does not require building anything.

Gate 3 is a **conversation**, not a contract.

---

## §9 — If Gate 3 Passes → Gate 4

Gate 4 = a paid pilot (see file 11). Only if Gate 3 PASS.

If Gate 3 PARTIAL → refine the message, try 3 more auditors.

If Gate 3 FAIL → Stop. Write a Stop Report. Reconsider.

**End of Gate 3 Playbook.**
